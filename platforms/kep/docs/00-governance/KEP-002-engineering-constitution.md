# 1. Purpose and Constitutional Authority

1.1. This Engineering Constitution establishes the binding engineering obligations of the Kuriosity Engineering Platform.

1.2. KEP-002 derives its constitutional mandate from KEP-000, which founded KEP, established its mission, principles, governance precedence, human-accountability requirements, and controlled-evolution authority.

1.3. Upon ratification, KEP-002 SHALL become the highest internal engineering-governance authority within KEP. It SHALL remain subordinate only to applicable law and binding contractual obligations.

1.4. KEP-000 SHALL remain the founding source and historical constitutional mandate of KEP. Where KEP-000 and KEP-002 appear inconsistent, the instruments SHALL be interpreted to preserve the founding mandate while giving controlling engineering-governance effect to KEP-002. A substantive irreconcilable conflict MUST be corrected through constitutional amendment.

1.5. This Constitution SHALL govern how intent, requirements, architecture, implementation, data, security, verification, release, operations, knowledge, human decisions, and AI-assisted work are controlled.

1.6. This Constitution SHALL NOT prescribe a vendor, programming language, cloud, repository host, operating system, framework, model, agent, or product architecture.

1.7. A subordinate instrument MAY define implementation mechanisms only within the authority and constraints established by this Constitution.

1.8. A tool default, generated artifact, repeated practice, prototype, undocumented convention, or implementation convenience MUST NOT acquire constitutional authority.

# 2. Constitutional Scope

2.1. This Constitution SHALL apply to all engineering work performed under KEP by humans, AI-assisted agents, autonomous or semi-autonomous systems, automation, repositories, products, projects, and platform capabilities.

2.2. Its lifecycle scope SHALL include intent, requirements, architecture, engineering contracts, execution, verification, review, delivery, release, operation, incident response, learning, migration, retirement, and decommissioning.

2.3. Its artifact scope SHALL include governance, requirements, decisions, designs, source code, configuration, schemas, data models, interfaces, infrastructure, tests, evidence, release records, runbooks, prompts, agent contracts, and operational records.

2.4. Products that adopt KEP SHALL remain accountable for their domain, product data, product-specific architecture, release lifecycle, security boundary, and operational outcomes.

2.5. KEP SHALL govern cross-product engineering rules and reusable capabilities without absorbing product-domain ownership.

2.6. A local product rule MAY be stricter than this Constitution but MUST NOT contradict a higher authority.

2.7. Conformance SHALL be proportional to system context and risk, but proportionality MUST NOT eliminate intent, accountability, truthfulness, or evidence.

# 3. Authority and Precedence

3.1. The following order of precedence SHALL govern KEP:

| Rank | Authority |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Applicable law and binding regulatory obligations |
| 2 | Binding contractual obligations |
| 3 | KEP-002 - Engineering Constitution |
| 4 | KEP-000 - Founding Charter |
| 5 | KEP-001 - Platform Scope, Boundaries, and Operating Model, together with incorporated founding records including KEP-001A |

| 7 | Approved architecture decisions |
| 8 | Project charters and approved requirements |
| 9 | Repository-level contracts and rules |
| 10 | Task-specific instructions |
| 11 | Tool, vendor, framework, model, or platform defaults |

3.2. A lower-level authority MUST NOT contradict, waive, or redefine a higher-level authority.

3.3. A conflict SHALL be resolved in favor of the higher authority unless an authorized exception is explicitly available and approved.

3.4. Silence in a higher authority MUST NOT be interpreted as permission to create a de facto constitutional rule.

3.5. Subordinate policies SHALL identify their governing authority, scope, owner, version, effective date, and amendment path.

3.6. KEP-001 and KEP-001A SHALL continue to govern platform boundaries, founding assignments, and resolved founding decisions except where validly superseded by KEP-002 or a later constitutional amendment.

3.7. UD-008 through UD-015 SHALL remain deferred. A subordinate or local decision MAY address a bounded need but MUST NOT be represented as KEP-wide policy.

# 4. Constitutional Engineering Duties

## 4.1. Duty of Intent

Material engineering work SHALL begin with an identifiable problem, intended outcome, scope, constraints, affected parties, and accountable owner.

## 4.2. Duty of Requirement Fidelity

Approved requirements MUST NOT be silently discarded, weakened, substituted, or materially reinterpreted.

## 4.3. Duty of Deliberate Architecture

Material architecture SHALL result from explicit evaluation of requirements, constraints, quality attributes, options, tradeoffs, risks, and expected evolution.

## 4.4. Duty of Domain Correctness

Systems SHALL preserve the domain meaning, terminology, invariants, state transitions, and ownership boundaries they are intended to represent.

## 4.5. Duty of Explicit Contracts

Material requirement, interface, data, security, operational, repository, and agent boundaries SHALL be governed by explicit contracts.

## 4.6. Duty of Evidence

Claims of completion, correctness, security, compatibility, performance, compliance, operability, or readiness MUST be supported by appropriate evidence.

## 4.7. Duty of Security

Security SHALL be addressed throughout the lifecycle and MUST NOT be deferred solely to final review.

## 4.8. Duty of Traceability

Material requirements, decisions, changes, evidence, approvals, exceptions, and releases SHALL remain traceable.

## 4.9. Duty of Operational Readiness

A system MUST NOT be declared production-ready solely because implementation or testing activity has stopped.

## 4.10. Duty of Maintainability

Engineering artifacts SHALL be understandable and safely modifiable by qualified contributors other than their original authors.

## 4.11. Duty of Honest Status

Assumptions, uncertainty, omissions, deferred work, failed or blocked checks, known defects, and unsupported claims MUST be stated explicitly.

## 4.12. Duty of Controlled Evolution

Material change SHALL evaluate compatibility, migration, security, data integrity, operations, recovery, documentation, affected contracts, and reconsideration triggers.

## 4.13. Duty of User Respect

Systems MUST NOT manipulate users through deceptive interfaces, hidden material behavior, or intentionally obscure consequences.

## 4.14. Duty of Sustainable Engineering

Urgency MAY justify temporary compromise only when the compromise is explicit, risk-assessed, owned, time-bounded where practical, and assigned for remediation.

# 5. Requirements Constitution

5.1. Every material requirement SHALL have a stable identifier, accountable owner, source, status, and validation method.

5.2. Requirements SHALL distinguish business outcomes, functional behavior, quality attributes, constraints, assumptions, exclusions, and acceptance criteria where applicable.

5.3. A material requirement MUST be testable or SHALL define another objective acceptance method.

5.4. Ambiguity, contradiction, and missing authority SHALL be raised and recorded rather than silently encoded into behavior.

5.5. Material requirement changes SHALL be versioned, impact-assessed, approved by the designated authority, and propagated to affected contracts, designs, tests, documentation, and release scope.

5.6. Implementation SHALL remain traceable to approved requirements and acceptance evidence.

5.7. A requirement SHALL NOT be considered satisfied merely because an implementation exists.

5.8. Conflicting requirements MUST be reconciled through authorized decision-making before dependent work is accepted.

5.9. Assumptions MAY support progress when explicitly recorded, bounded, assigned an owner, and paired with a confirmation or reconsideration condition.

5.10. Product-domain requirements SHALL remain owned by the product authority unless a higher authority explicitly assigns otherwise.

# 6. Architecture Constitution

6.1. Architecture SHALL align with approved requirements, domain boundaries, quality attributes, risk, operational context, and expected evolution.

6.2. Domain models and boundaries SHOULD reflect business meaning rather than arbitrary technical divisions.

6.3. High-level business policy SHOULD NOT depend directly on volatile infrastructure where a controlled boundary can preserve clarity and replaceability.

6.4. Separation of concerns SHALL be introduced when it improves clarity, security, testability, maintainability, or independent evolution; it MUST NOT be introduced solely to imitate a pattern.

6.5. Interfaces between material components SHALL define inputs, outputs, validation, authorization, errors, versioning, compatibility, observability, and idempotency where applicable.

6.6. Architectures SHALL account for applicable failure modes, including invalid data, dependency failure, timeout, partial completion, duplicate requests, network interruption, concurrency, rate limits, authentication expiration, resource exhaustion, deployment failure, and recovery.

6.7. External technologies and vendors SHOULD be isolated behind controlled boundaries where replacement or lock-in risk is material.

6.8. Distributed architecture, shared runtime services, and platform centralization MUST be justified by evidence and SHALL NOT be treated as default signs of maturity.

6.9. Reuse SHALL be earned through coherent abstraction, demonstrated demand, ownership, compatibility, support, and reduced total cost or risk.

6.10. Material architecture decisions SHALL record context, decision, alternatives, rationale, consequences, owner, validation, and reconsideration trigger.

6.11. Architecture drift SHALL be treated as a defect and MUST be corrected, approved through a new decision, or covered by an authorized exception.

6.12. Architecture standards SHALL remain technology-neutral at the constitutional level.

# 7. Data Constitution

7.1. Every material dataset SHALL have an identifiable owner, defined meaning, classification, lifecycle, and system of record.

7.2. Data contracts SHALL define structure, semantics, quality expectations, ownership, permitted use, compatibility, retention dependencies, and error behavior as applicable.

7.3. Data collection, access, replication, and retention SHALL be limited to legitimate authorized need.

7.4. Data duplication across systems SHALL require a stated purpose, synchronization model, consistency expectation, conflict rule, recovery procedure, and exit path.

7.5. Schema changes SHALL be versioned and evaluated for compatibility, migration, rollback or recovery, downstream consumers, and data integrity.

7.6. A data migration SHALL include verification appropriate to risk and MUST NOT be declared successful without evidence.

7.7. Production data MUST NOT be used in development, test, or AI contexts without authorization and controls appropriate to its classification.

7.8. Audit-critical data SHALL preserve attribution, integrity, chronology, and provenance appropriate to its purpose.

7.9. Deletion and decommissioning SHALL respect applicable legal, contractual, retention, recovery, and evidentiary obligations.

7.10. This Constitution does not establish the canonical evidence store, universal retention period, or integrity mechanism reserved under UD-009.

# 8. Security Constitution

8.1. Security SHALL be treated as a system property spanning requirements, architecture, implementation, data, integration, deployment, operation, incident response, and decommissioning.

8.2. Access SHALL follow least privilege, explicit authorization, separation of duties where justified, and secure defaults.

8.3. Authentication, authorization, confidentiality, integrity, auditability, and trust boundaries SHALL be explicit where applicable.

8.4. Secrets and sensitive configuration MUST NOT be embedded in source, logs, prompts, generated artifacts, or other unauthorized locations.

8.5. Inputs, outputs, files, messages, and external responses SHALL be validated and handled according to their trust level.

8.6. Dependencies and delivery pipelines SHALL be managed as supply-chain risk and subjected to controls proportional to change and system risk.

8.7. Security-relevant logging SHALL avoid unnecessary exposure of secrets, credentials, regulated data, and sensitive context.

8.8. Environments SHALL be separated to the extent required by risk, data classification, and operational consequence.

8.9. Vulnerabilities SHALL be triaged, owned, remediated or accepted by authorized authority, and tracked to closure.

8.10. Security incidents SHALL trigger containment, evidence preservation, accountable escalation, recovery, and learning appropriate to impact.

8.11. Security claims MUST NOT be inferred solely from framework defaults, tool output, AI explanation, or absence of known incidents.

8.12. This Constitution establishes security obligations but does not select the platform-wide identity, authorization, signing, or provenance model reserved under UD-008.

# 9. Verification Constitution

9.1. No material deliverable SHALL be accepted without evidence appropriate to its requirements, contracts, risk, and intended use.

9.2. Verification SHALL distinguish pass, fail, blocked, not run, and not applicable states.

9.3. A check that was not executed MUST NOT be reported or implied as passed.

9.4. Verification evidence SHOULD be attributable, reproducible where practical, connected to the evaluated artifact and version, and protected from inappropriate alteration.

9.5. Applicable evidence MAY include build results, automated tests, contract tests, static analysis, security scans, runtime checks, migration validation, performance measurements, accessibility evaluation, deployment verification, operational-readiness review, acceptance validation, and authorized human approval.

9.6. Verification SHALL test claims and contracts; it MUST NOT be reduced to maximizing pass rates or producing ceremonial evidence.

9.7. Failed mandatory gates SHALL block progression unless an exception is constitutionally permitted and approved.

9.8. Findings SHALL identify the affected artifact, issue, impact, governing rule, evidence, severity, owner, and required disposition.

9.9. Verification reduces uncertainty but SHALL NOT be represented as a guarantee of defect absence.

9.10. Evidence requirements SHALL scale with R0-R4 risk and MAY be raised when later findings show the original classification was insufficient.

# 10. Release Constitution

10.1. Every material release SHALL have a defined scope, version or identity, accountable owner, assigned risk class, required gates, release authority, and durable release record.

10.2. A release SHALL include or reference approved requirements, change impact, verification evidence, known risks, configuration expectations, deployment procedure, recovery or rollback approach, documentation updates, and required approvals.

10.3. A release MUST NOT proceed when a mandatory gate has failed, required evidence is absent, or the release authority is not established, except through an authorized exception available under this Constitution.

10.4. Known limitations, unresolved defects, deferred work, and compatibility effects SHALL be disclosed to affected stakeholders.

10.5. Breaking changes SHALL require explicit versioning, impact analysis, migration guidance, and approval appropriate to risk.

10.6. Production release evidence SHALL reflect the artifact and configuration actually released.

10.7. Emergency releases MAY use compressed process only under Section 18, but truthfulness, accountability, evidence preservation, and post-event review SHALL remain mandatory.

10.8. No release mechanism, repository host, deployment platform, or vendor is selected by this Constitution.

# 11. Operations Constitution

11.1. Every production system, service, repository process, and approved platform service SHALL have identifiable operational ownership.

11.2. Operational contracts SHALL define applicable service expectations, dependencies, support boundaries, monitoring, incident handling, recovery, and change responsibilities.

11.3. Production systems SHALL expose sufficient observability to determine what happened, when, where, by whom or what, and which business or platform operation was affected.

11.4. Observability SHOULD include structured logs, metrics, traces, audit records, correlation identifiers, health checks, and alerts as appropriate to risk and architecture.

11.5. Runbooks, configuration references, recovery procedures, and incident procedures SHALL be maintained where operational consequence justifies them.

11.6. Backup and restoration SHALL be defined and tested where data loss or service interruption creates material risk.

11.7. Operational changes SHALL be traceable and controlled according to risk.

11.8. Incidents SHALL produce learning proportionate to impact, including corrective actions and platform feedback where the lesson generalizes.

11.9. Decommissioning SHALL address data, dependencies, access, configuration, monitoring, records, contractual obligations, and stakeholder communication.

11.10. This Constitution does not authorize a mandatory shared runtime service; that matter remains deferred under UD-012.

# 12. AI Engineering Constitution

12.1. AI SHALL be treated as an engineering participant and MUST NOT be treated as the accountable authority for material business, architecture, security, release, legal, or production outcomes.

12.2. Every material AI-assisted task SHALL operate under an explicit agent contract or task envelope defining objective, permitted scope, prohibited actions, authoritative sources, expected outputs, validation, completion criteria, escalation, and human authority.

12.3. AI agents SHALL receive sufficient governed context, including applicable scope, requirements, architecture, repository rules, domain terminology, security constraints, task boundaries, and definition of done.

12.4. AI output SHALL be subjected to the same applicable requirements, security, review, and verification standards as human-produced work.

12.5. An AI agent MUST NOT claim to have read a file, executed a command, run a test, inspected a result, consulted an authority, changed a system, or completed work unless that action occurred and is supported by available evidence.

12.6. AI agents SHALL distinguish verified fact, source-derived statement, inference, assumption, recommendation, uncertainty, and incomplete work.

12.7. AI agents MUST NOT silently resolve a material ambiguity, conflict of authority, missing requirement, or deferred constitutional decision.

12.8. AI agents MUST NOT perform destructive, irreversible, security-sensitive, regulated, identity-affecting, financial, production-impacting, or release-authorizing actions without explicit applicable authority and controls.

12.9. Material AI-assisted decisions SHALL retain sufficient context to identify the assigned task, authoritative sources, assumptions, output, validation, and human approval where required.

12.10. AI recommendation authority SHALL remain separate from human approval and accountability authority.

12.11. Agent and model replaceability SHOULD be preserved through tool-neutral contracts and controlled adapters where practical.

12.12. This Constitution does not establish universal agent-execution logging scope, sensitive-context rules, or retention periods reserved under UD-010.

# 13. Human Accountability

13.1. Every material system, repository, platform capability, decision, release, exception, and operational process SHALL have an accountable human owner.

13.2. Delegation of execution SHALL NOT transfer accountability unless a formal ownership change is recorded.

13.3. AI, automation, committees without named authority, and shared responsibility statements MUST NOT replace identifiable human accountability.

13.4. Human authorities SHALL make decisions within their assigned scope and MUST disclose material conflicts, uncertainty, and limitations.

13.5. Review SHALL focus on artifacts, decisions, risks, contracts, and evidence and MUST NOT become personal, territorial, retaliatory, or performative.

13.6. Contributors SHALL be permitted and expected to report material risk, nonconformance, uncertainty, and failed evidence without concealment.

13.7. One person MAY hold multiple roles during founding-stage operation as established by KEP-001A. Role separation SHOULD increase when scale, risk, independence, contributor count, or conflict exposure makes separation materially beneficial.

13.8. This Constitution does not establish the formal conflict-of-interest or appeal process reserved under UD-014.

# 14. Risk Constitution

14.1. Every material change SHALL be assigned an initial risk class before release and SHOULD be classified before implementation begins.

| Class | Constitutional Definition | Minimum Governance Principle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R0 | Editorial or no runtime impact. | Integrity check and proportionate review. |
| R1 | Low-risk localized change. | Localized verification and accountable owner review. |
| R2 | Moderate functional or integration change. | Requirement, test, integration, compatibility, and review gates proportional to impact. |
| R3 | High-risk security, data, architecture, migration, or production change. | Expanded architecture, security, recovery, migration, and release evidence; independent review where practical. |
| R4 | Critical legal, financial, safety, identity, regulated-data, or irreversible change. | Maximum applicable controls, explicit accountable approval, independent evidence, and documented rollback or explicit demonstration that rollback is impossible. |

14.2. Risk SHALL be classified by potential impact, reversibility, exposure, and uncertainty rather than implementation effort or schedule pressure.

14.3. Risk classification MAY be raised at any stage when evidence indicates greater impact or uncertainty.

14.4. Risk classification MUST NOT be lowered merely to reduce review, evidence, approval, or schedule burden.

14.5. Quality gates, review independence, evidence depth, release authority, and exception authority SHALL scale with risk.

14.6. R3 and R4 changes SHOULD receive independent review when independence is practicable and SHALL record why independence was unavailable when material.

14.7. R4 changes SHALL require explicit accountable approval and MUST NOT rely solely on automated or AI-generated evidence.

14.8. Numeric thresholds, quantitative baselines, and performance targets are not established by this Constitution and remain deferred under UD-011.

# 15. Exception Constitution

15.1. An exception MAY be requested only when compliance would create disproportionate cost, risk, technical harm, or conflict with a higher authority.

15.2. An exception SHALL identify the exact rule, reason, affected scope, risk class, risks introduced, compensating controls, owner, approving authority, effective period, review or expiry date, and exit or remediation conditions.

15.3. An undocumented deviation is not an approved exception.

15.4. An exception MUST NOT override applicable law, binding contractual obligations, human accountability, truthful evidence, or constitutional amendment requirements.

15.5. Exceptions SHALL be time-bounded where practical and MUST NOT become permanent hidden rules.

15.6. Expired exceptions SHALL cease to authorize deviation unless renewed through the applicable process.

15.7. Repeated exceptions SHALL trigger review of the underlying rule, system design, capability placement, or adoption model.

15.8. Exception authority SHALL scale with risk and SHALL be separated from execution authority where required by subordinate policy.

15.9. A constitutional exception MUST NOT be created by ordinary architecture decision, repository rule, product requirement, or task instruction.

# 16. Knowledge Constitution

16.1. Critical engineering knowledge SHALL be durable, versioned where appropriate, discoverable, attributable, and accessible to authorized humans and agents.

16.2. A repository SHALL operate as an engineering knowledge base and MUST explain how the governed system is understood, changed, verified, released, operated, recovered, and retired.

16.3. Projects SHALL identify authoritative sources for requirements, architecture, interfaces, data, security, deployment, operations, and agent instructions.

16.4. Search results, summaries, generated text, and AI memory MUST NOT be treated as authority unless they resolve to an authoritative source.

16.5. Material decisions SHALL be recorded with context, alternatives, rationale, consequences, status, date, owner, validation, and reconsideration trigger.

16.6. Documentation that materially conflicts with implemented behavior or governing contracts is a defect.

16.7. Documentation SHALL be updated as part of the same controlled change that alters the behavior it describes.

16.8. Knowledge MUST NOT remain exclusively in a private conversation, personal memory, untracked prompt, local machine, inaccessible service, or transient agent session.

16.9. Conflicting authoritative artifacts SHALL be reconciled rather than left ambiguous.

16.10. Artifacts without a clear purpose, owner, authority, or current use SHOULD be removed, archived, or explicitly marked.

# 17. Constitutional Nonconformance

| Class | Definition | Required Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constitutional breach | Violation of KEP-002 or a higher authority that creates or could create material legal, contractual, security, financial, safety, identity, regulated-data, irreversible, governance, or human-accountability impact. | Immediate escalation; block or suspend affected release or operation where necessary; preserve evidence; assign accountable owner; remediate or pursue constitutional action. |
| Major nonconformance | Material violation of a binding requirement, architecture, security, data, verification, release, operational, or accountability obligation. | Block progression unless authorized exception exists; corrective action and verification required. |
| Minor nonconformance | Limited violation with low immediate impact that does not invalidate the principal outcome. | Record, assign, correct within an approved period, and verify closure. |
| Observation | Potential weakness, ambiguity, or improvement not yet shown to violate a binding obligation. | Record when useful; evaluate through normal governance. |
| Approved exception | Authorized, bounded deviation meeting Section 15. | Operate only within approved scope, controls, and period. |

17.1. Severity SHALL be based on impact, scope, reversibility, authority violated, and evidence, not reviewer preference.

17.2. A constitutional breach involving R4 impact SHALL trigger immediate accountable escalation.

17.3. A nonconformance MUST NOT be concealed, relabeled, or closed without evidence of correction, accepted risk, authorized exception, or valid superseding decision.

17.4. Approval of an artifact SHALL NOT remove the artifact owner's accountability or prevent later reclassification when new evidence emerges.

17.5. Conformance reports SHALL disclose material dependency on unresolved or deferred areas.

# 18. Ratification, Amendment, Interpretation, and Emergency Controls

## 18.1 Ratification

18.1.1. KEP-002 Version 1.0 SHALL become binding when ratified by the Founding Authority through signature or an equivalent durable approved record and when its effective date is recorded.

18.1.2. The canonical KEP repository SHALL record the ratified instrument as authoritative and identify applicable law and binding contractual obligations as superior authorities.

18.1.3. Ratification SHALL NOT imply that subordinate mechanisms or deferred decisions have been approved.

## 18.2 Amendment

18.2.1. A change affecting constitutional authority, engineering duties, precedence, human accountability, risk classes, amendment rules, or constitutional commitments SHALL require a constitutional amendment.

18.2.2. An amendment proposal SHALL include the problem, authority, affected sections, rationale, alternatives, risk, compatibility impact, migration effect, implementation obligations, review evidence, and effective date.

18.2.3. Editorial corrections that do not change meaning MAY be issued through controlled revision by the Governance Steward, with a revision record and verification that normative meaning is unchanged.

18.2.4. A constitutional amendment SHALL be versioned and ratified by the constitutionally designated authority.

18.2.5. A constitutional amendment that breaks existing governed contracts SHALL provide migration guidance or explicitly record why migration cannot be preserved.

## 18.3 Interpretation

18.3.1. The Founding Authority SHALL serve as the initial constitutional interpretation authority until a later ratified governance instrument assigns that role.

18.3.2. Interpretation SHALL preserve the text, purpose, authority hierarchy, product independence, human accountability, and deferred-decision boundaries of this Constitution.

18.3.3. Interpretation MUST NOT create a permanent new constitutional obligation that properly requires amendment.

18.3.4. Material interpretations SHALL be recorded as durable, versioned decisions and reviewed for incorporation into the next controlled constitutional revision.

## 18.4 Emergency Controls

18.4.1. An accountable authority MAY impose a temporary emergency control when delay would create material legal, contractual, security, safety, data, financial, identity, availability, or irreversible risk.

18.4.2. An emergency control SHALL identify the triggering condition, scope, authority, owner, risk, actions permitted, actions prohibited, duration, evidence requirements, and review time.

18.4.3. Emergency action MAY compress normal process but MUST preserve truthfulness, authorization, evidence, least privilege, reversibility where possible, and human accountability.

18.4.4. Emergency authority MUST NOT be used to create a permanent architecture, policy, exception, or constitutional amendment.

18.4.5. An emergency action SHALL receive post-event review and formal disposition as soon as practical.

# 19. Constitutional Commitments

19.1. No engineering activity SHALL override applicable law or binding contractual obligations.

19.2. No material requirement SHALL be silently discarded.

19.3. No material architecture SHALL exist solely by accident or undocumented preference.

19.4. No system SHALL claim correctness, security, readiness, or completion without appropriate evidence.

19.5. No AI-generated output SHALL bypass applicable validation or human accountability.

19.6. No release SHALL proceed through concealed gate failure or fabricated evidence.

19.7. No critical data SHALL exist without ownership, meaning, protection, and lifecycle control.

19.8. No production system SHALL operate without accountable ownership.

19.9. No significant exception SHALL remain undocumented or become a permanent hidden rule.

19.10. No unresolved constitutional matter SHALL be settled by implementation convenience.

19.11. No contributor or agent SHALL claim work, access, execution, or verification that did not occur.

19.12. No project SHALL sacrifice long-term system integrity without an explicit, controlled, risk-assessed, and reviewable decision.

19.13. No platform capability SHALL absorb product-domain ownership without explicit authority.

19.14. No lower authority SHALL override this Constitution.

19.15. No implementation mechanism SHALL be confused with a constitutional obligation.
