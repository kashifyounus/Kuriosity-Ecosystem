## 1. Purpose

This model defines accountable KE roles, their authority boundaries, and the controls governing delegation and separation of responsibility.

## 2. Authority Rules

- Governing authority shall be explicit and traceable to an approved instrument.
- Every normative artifact, platform, release, and material decision shall have one accountable human owner.
- Execution may be delegated; accountability may be transferred only through an approved record.
- Review, verification, repository maintenance, and automation do not imply approval authority.
- One person may hold multiple roles during the founding stage when the assignments and conflicts are visible.
- A role shall not exercise authority outside the scope granted by superior governance.

## 3. Foundational Roles

### 3.1 Founding Authority

The Founding Authority may ratify founding KE governance, assign initial accountable roles, and establish a successor constitutional authority within KE-000.

The Founding Authority shall not use subordinate artifacts to bypass constitutional amendment controls.

### 3.2 Ecosystem Governance Authority

The Ecosystem Governance Authority owns subordinate KE governance, ecosystem-wide policies, admission and retirement controls, and cross-platform governance consistency.

### 3.3 Ecosystem Architecture Authority

The Ecosystem Architecture Authority owns ecosystem-level architectural coherence, platform boundary review, cross-platform dependency review, and KE-level architecture decisions.

It does not own platform-internal architecture or product architecture except where an approved KE boundary is affected.

### 3.4 Platform Owner

Each Platform Owner is accountable for the platform mandate, governance, release integrity, adoption contract, lifecycle, and compatibility obligations.

A Platform Owner does not own adopting product business decisions.

### 3.5 Product Owner

Each Product Owner is accountable for product purpose, business requirements, domain decisions, product compliance, product adoption choices, deviations, and product lifecycle.

A Product Owner does not redefine ecosystem or platform authority.

### 3.6 Standards Authority

The Standards Authority owns the lifecycle, consistency, versioning, and approval routing of standards within its assigned authority boundary.

### 3.7 Release Authority

The Release Authority approves a declared release after verifying the normative inventory, evidence, dependencies, known limitations, and adoption coordinates.

Publication mechanics do not substitute for release approval.

### 3.8 Verification Authority

The Verification Authority evaluates evidence against approved criteria and records pass, fail, blocked, or conditional outcomes.

The Verification Authority shall not convert an unapproved proposal into governing authority.

### 3.9 Repository Maintainer

The Repository Maintainer preserves repository structure, branch controls, artifact integrity, cross-references, manifests, and publication evidence.

The Repository Maintainer may reject mechanically invalid publication but does not independently approve normative meaning.

### 3.10 AI and Automation Participant

AI and automation may analyze, draft, validate, transform, and report within an assigned task.

AI and automation shall not independently ratify authority, approve deviations, assume human accountability, or represent proposals as approved.

## 4. Responsibility Assignment

For every material governed activity, the controlling artifact shall identify:

- the accountable role;
- the execution role or participant;
- required reviewers;
- required approver;
- required evidence; and
- escalation authority.

The same person may occupy several roles where permitted, but the distinct responsibilities shall remain visible.

## 5. Conflict of Interest and Independence

Where risk, law, contract, security, financial exposure, or irreversible impact requires independent review, the accountable authority shall assign a reviewer who did not author or execute the controlled change.

If independence cannot be achieved during the founding stage, the limitation and compensating controls shall be recorded before approval.

## 6. Delegation and Succession

A delegation record shall state the delegating authority, delegate, scope, duration, retained accountability, and revocation conditions.

A permanent transfer of accountability requires an approved ownership or succession record and updates to all affected authoritative artifacts.
