## 1. Purpose

This Constitution establishes the highest internal governing authority of the Kuriosity Ecosystem, hereafter referred to as KE. It defines the enduring principles, authority hierarchy, governance boundaries, and constitutional change controls by which KE remains coherent, accountable, and capable of controlled evolution.

## 2. Mission

KE exists to provide a coherent, reusable, and governed ecosystem through which independent platforms enable products while preserving explicit authority, accountable ownership, trustworthy evidence, and durable engineering knowledge.

## 3. Scope

This Constitution governs KE as a whole.

It binds:

- ecosystem governance and authorities;
- every KE platform and shared capability;
- every product insofar as it adopts or claims conformance with KE;
- every normative KE asset and decision; and
- every human or automated participant acting under KE authority.

This Constitution does not define platform-specific governance, product-specific governance, business-domain rules, technology choices, implementation methods, or repository procedures.

Applicable law and binding contractual obligations remain superior to this Constitution.

## 4. Constitutional Principles

### 4.1 Ecosystem Supremacy

KE governs the ecosystem as a whole. No platform, product, capability, asset, authority, or participant may redefine KE or claim authority superior to this Constitution.

### 4.2 Separation of Authority

The ecosystem governs shared ecosystem concerns. Platforms govern reusable capabilities within their approved mandates. Products govern their business domains. Authority shall not be transferred across these boundaries by implication.

### 4.3 Composition Without Duplication

Products adopt platforms through composition. Products shall not redefine platform authority, and platforms shall not absorb product-domain authority.

### 4.4 Bounded Autonomy

Every platform and product remains autonomous within its approved scope and subordinate to the authorities that govern that scope.

### 4.5 Explicit Accountability

Every normative decision, governed asset, platform, product, and shared capability shall have identifiable human accountability. Delegation of work does not remove accountability.

### 4.6 Evidence Before Authority Claims

Claims of approval, conformance, completion, quality, or readiness shall be supported by durable and reviewable evidence.

### 4.7 Durable Knowledge

Stable decisions and governing knowledge shall be preserved in authoritative, versioned, and discoverable records. Conversation, memory, convention, and automated output are not governing authority unless incorporated through an authorized process.

### 4.8 Controlled Evolution

KE shall evolve through explicit, traceable, and versioned change. Practice, implementation, precedent, automation, or repeated use shall not amend governing authority implicitly.

### 4.9 Simplicity and Reuse

KE shall prefer coherent reuse and the minimum sufficient governing structure. New concepts shall not be introduced where an existing concept can be extended without loss of meaning or authority.

### 4.10 Human Authority Over Automation

Automation and artificial intelligence may assist KE but shall not independently create, ratify, transfer, or supersede governing authority. Accountable human authority remains final.

### 4.11 Technology Neutrality

KE constitutional authority is independent of any technology, vendor, tool, implementation, or delivery mechanism.

### 4.12 Integrity of the Whole

No local decision may compromise the coherence, security, traceability, maintainability, or governed boundaries of KE as a whole.

## 5. Authority Hierarchy

Authority within KE shall be interpreted in the following descending order:

1. applicable law and binding legal obligations;
2. binding contracts;
3. this Constitution;
4. the KE Founding Charter;
5. the KE Operating Model;
6. approved KE authority models and policies;
7. approved KE standards;
8. approved architecture decisions;
9. approved platform governance;
10. approved product governance;
11. approved implementation decisions; and
12. implementation artifacts and operational practice.

A lower authority shall not contradict a higher authority. Where authorities at the same level conflict, the more specifically applicable approved authority governs; unresolved conflict shall be escalated to the next superior authority and shall not be settled by implementation.

## 6. Governance Boundaries

### 6.1 Ecosystem Boundary

KE retains authority over ecosystem identity, constitutional principles, authority composition, cross-ecosystem governance, platform admission, ecosystem-wide standards, and the rules by which platforms and products participate in KE.

### 6.2 Platform Boundary

A platform retains authority over reusable capabilities within its approved mandate. A platform shall not define KE constitutional authority or a product's business domain.

### 6.3 Product Boundary

A product retains authority over its business purpose, domain, requirements, data, operations, compliance obligations, and product lifecycle. A product shall not redefine KE or platform authority.

### 6.4 Capability Boundary

A capability shall be governed by one accountable owner within an approved authority boundary. Shared use does not create shared or ambiguous ownership.

### 6.5 Adoption and Deviation Boundary

Adoption of KE or a KE platform creates obligations only within the declared scope of adoption. A deviation shall be explicit, bounded, approved by the competent authority, and subordinate to this Constitution.

### 6.6 Non-Transfer by Silence

Authority, ownership, approval, and responsibility shall not arise through silence, convenience, automation, implementation precedent, or absence of objection.

## 7. Constitutional Change Process

This Constitution may be changed only through a constitutional amendment.

A constitutional amendment shall:

1. identify the provision and constitutional need;
2. state the proposed change and its consequences;
3. assess conflicts with superior obligations and effects on subordinate authorities;
4. preserve traceability to the prior constitutional state;
5. receive explicit approval from the Founding Authority or a duly established successor constitutional authority;
6. be recorded as a versioned, durable, and reviewable instrument; and
7. state its effective date and transition effect.

Editorial corrections that do not change meaning or authority may be approved as a non-substantive revision. Any change to a principle, hierarchy, boundary, amendment control, or allocation of constitutional authority is substantive and requires constitutional ratification.

No architecture decision, policy, standard, platform rule, product rule, implementation, automated action, or customary practice may amend this Constitution.
